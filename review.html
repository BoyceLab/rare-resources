Families, siblings and everyday support

These needs deserve separate entry points. Parents may be looking for service navigation or education support; siblings may want peer connection, age-specific groups or help planning an adult role. Caregivers may need respite, mental-health support, feeding guidance or help coordinating complex care.

Start with Sibling Support Project, Sibling Leadership Network, Parent to Parent USA and Family Voices' local directory. The directory also includes UK and Australian sibling services, parent centers, rare-disease caregiver support, family camps and condition-specific family groups.

The review of psychosocial support for siblings is useful for considering program goals and evidence limitations. A peer group should be described as peer support; access to a group does not establish a mental-health treatment benefit. The respite review and Caregiving in the US 2025 add context for family needs, while local eligibility still requires direct checking.

Transition is several connected tasks

Adult healthcare, education, work, benefits, housing and decision support need their own planning. Use a healthcare-transition tool alongside the relevant education, benefits and independent-living resources.

Got Transition shows current activity through an October 7, 2026 webinar and current guidance. Its Six Core Elements, youth quiz and family toolkit remain useful starting points. Older grant language in a toolkit should not be read as current funding confirmation.

The directory includes Moving to Adult Health Care, PACER, NTACT:C, Think College, Project SEARCH, vocational rehabilitation directories, Social Security youth guidance, supported decision-making tools and independent-living directories. The 2018 transition report provides clinical context; current service routes and rules need newer official sources.

Communication and AAC across the lifespan

Look across user-led communities, clinical assessment, communication-partner learning, literacy, alternative access and equipment funding. A device vendor is one part of that picture. The directory keeps organizations and practical supports visible alongside commercial products and research programs.

The ASHA AAC Practice Portal and Communication Bill of Rights provide useful starting points. The reading list includes intervention trials, user perspectives, eye-gaze research and adult communication studies. Results from a particular population or intervention should not be generalized to every AAC user. Experimental speech neuroprostheses are distinct from routinely available communication services.

Tools for patient organizations

Useful organizational support extends beyond fundraising. Governance, research partnerships, community engagement, informed consent, compensation, data sharing and research planning all need attention.

Boyce Data Science provides practical tools including Patient Advocacy Group in a Box, The Well-Run Board, The First-Time Funder, Data Sharing in a Box and a registry demonstration. Individual draft and demonstration labels are retained. Other entry points include NORD RareLaunch, Global Genes RARE University and Data DIY, EURORDIS Open Academy, EUPATI, NCATS, PCORI, the National Council of Nonprofits and Candid.

Training, a planning template and an open funding opportunity are different resources. The directory identifies closed application cycles and future intake dates where those are stated. An archived course or published workbook can remain useful without implying that live enrollment is open.

Choose the registry role before the platform

The registry comparison includes 42 options across several roles. It is intended to help form a shortlist and questions for providers. It does not rank products or verify security, regulatory compliance or contract terms.

Across Healthcare Matrix describes organization-specific deployments. It is separate from the AAC assessment called Communication Matrix. RARE-X describes community data collection and controlled research access; participation and research access have distinct processes.

Before seeking proposals, agree on the community's question, governance, data to collect, burden, languages, accessibility, outcome measures, staffing and operating budget. Ask each provider about data ownership and export, consent and withdrawal, pediatric-to-adult reconsent, EHR coverage, data quality, research access, complete costs, migration and closure planning.

RaDaR supports registry planning. The FDA registry guidance addresses regulatory use, which is a more specific goal than collecting useful community information. Technical standards support a design; they do not by themselves establish the quality of a study.

How to interpret this review

The directory distinguishes website access, program activity and review depth. A retrieved page can be out of date; a blocked page can belong to an active organization. Current enrollment and funding are recorded only when supported by the reviewed material.

The review used official program and publisher pages, related-resource discovery and targeted searches. It has a United States and English-language emphasis. It did not use systematic-review screening, exhaustive database searches or formal risk-of-bias assessment.

Of 936 entries, 205 received focused description and status review in this expansion, 170 have reviewed descriptions, and 561 retain basic descriptions needing further editorial review. Each entry shows its review level. Read the detailed method.
